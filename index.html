How I turn data into decisions

Core areas from my B.Tech (AI & ML) coursework and applied in projects like the crop yield predictor — from raw data to a working, evaluated model.

In plain terms

Machine learning is just teaching a computer to spot patterns in past data so it can make useful predictions about new situations. In my crop yield project, that means: feeding it years of weather and soil records, letting it learn which conditions lead to a good or bad harvest, and then asking it to predict next season's yield — so farmers can plan better. The five steps below show how any of my ML projects go from raw data to a working prediction.

Agricultural Production & Crop Yield Prediction System

          
An end-to-end machine-learning system designed to estimate crop yield from soil conditions, weather patterns, location, crop type, and historical production records. The goal is to turn scattered agricultural data into understandable planning insights.

          

            
ProblemYield planning is difficult when soil, rainfall, temperature, and historical production data are considered separately.

            
SolutionA reusable ML pipeline that cleans the data, builds features, compares models, and produces yield estimates and visual trends.

            
InputsCrop, season, area, soil indicators, rainfall, temperature, location, and previous production records.

            
EvaluationModel comparison with MAE, RMSE, R², residual analysis, and predicted-versus-actual visualizations.
